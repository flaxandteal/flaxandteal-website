---

We work with organisations that hold heritage records they cannot afford to lose.

Arches is an open source data management platform for cultural heritage, jointly funded by the World Monuments Fund and the Getty Conservation Institute, and it has become the platform of choice for historic environment records and heritage agencies internationally. We are registered Arches providers, and we do the work around it: migrating decades of existing records into it, modelling the data properly, integrating it with the systems around it, hosting it, and supporting the people who use it every day.

The part that matters most is usually the least visible. Getting the data model right, keeping provenance intact, and making sure a record entered today is still findable and still trustworthy in twenty years.

---

Most of our conversations start from one of these:

* Records spread across ageing databases, spreadsheets and shared drives, with no single reliable version
* A database that has outgrown itself, or that only one person understands
* A migration that has to happen without losing a single record, and without pausing day-to-day work
* Small teams with no dedicated IT, who need something that keeps working without constant attention
* Sensitive site locations that need genuine access control, not just a login screen
* Obligations to connect to national registers, planning systems or funder data requirements
* A grant-funded project that will end, and records that need to survive it

Resistance to changing a working process is reasonable, not an obstacle. A migration that disrupts the team it is meant to help has failed, whatever the data says.

---

**Start small**

A hosted Arches instance you can put real records into and actually use, priced to be straightforward to approve. The point is to find out whether the platform fits your workflow before anyone commits to a programme of work.

**Discovery**

A scoped piece of work to establish what you hold, what condition it is in, what the data model needs to be, and what a full implementation would realistically involve. Delivered in stages, so you get a usable answer early and can stop there if you want to.

**Full implementation**

Custom Arches deployments for national and large-scale records: data modelling, migration, bespoke workflows, dashboards built around your actual business processes, and secure development, staging and production instances.

**Hosting and support**

Hosted on-premise or in the cloud, with a proper support channel and defined response commitments. Not a mailbox someone checks when they can.

---

**A national historic environment record**

A long-term digital transformation for a Northern Ireland public sector body, migrating all current and historic heritage data into a customised Arches HER instance. More than 200,000 records, moved without data loss. Site visits, workshops and detailed data modelling on the human side; Kubernetes, Python in the cloud and Ray data workflows on the technical side.

**A state government heritage register**

A public-facing heritage register delivered for a state government department in Australia, now live and in daily public use.

**International research infrastructure**

Arches deployed and hosted as the data repository for a large cross-disciplinary research programme on urban policy and disaster risk, spanning institutions in Scotland, Nepal, Kenya and Turkey, with UKRI-compliant data management. This meant interviewing social scientists and geologists alike to find one shared set of requirements.

**Contributing upstream**

Working with a major national science museum, we extended Arches' Bulk Data Manager so users could edit, flag and bulk-upload data themselves, and are integrating that work back into the wider Arches codebase.

**European heritage digital twins**

We are a partner in a Horizon Europe consortium building interoperable digital twins of heritage sites, responsible for the semantic modelling and data management layer, working to CIDOC-CRM and European heritage cloud interoperability standards.

We have also hosted the international Arches developer meetup, and have run community events combining Arches with virtual reality to push what the platform can do.

The software is free. What is not free is the twenty years of records you need to move into it without losing anything, the data model that has to reflect how your organisation actually works, the integrations, the hosting, and someone who answers the phone in year three when the person who set it up has moved on.

* **We are registered Arches providers**, and we work directly with the Arches core team. We ran the workshops that built a roadmap for integrating Arches with modern DevOps practice, and we were the leading team doing so.

* **We have done this at national scale**, and at small-team scale, and we do not confuse the two. What a 200,000-record national register needs is not what a small record office needs.
