Then you probably want to know what we do around it rather than what it is. Data modelling, migration at scale, custom workflows, dashboards built around your actual business processes, integration with the systems either side, and hosting with a support channel that answers.

We are registered Arches providers, we work directly with the Arches core team, and we contribute our improvements back to the platform.

---

You already know your records. What tends to go wrong is underneath them.

Getting the data model right, so that the way your organisation actually works is reflected rather than fought. Keeping provenance intact, so a record can still be traced back years later. Making sure something entered today is still findable and still trustworthy decades from now, by someone who never met the person who entered it.

That is the work. The platform is how we do it, not what we are selling.

---

Most conversations start from one of these:

* Records spread across ageing databases, spreadsheets and shared drives, with no single reliable version
* A database that has outgrown itself, or that only one person understands
* A migration that has to happen without losing a single record, and without pausing day-to-day work
* A small team with no dedicated IT, needing something that keeps working without constant attention
* Sensitive site locations that need genuine access control, not just a login screen
* Obligations to connect to national registers, planning systems or funder requirements
* A grant-funded project that will end, and records that need to survive it

Reluctance to change a process that currently works is reasonable, not an obstacle. A migration that disrupts the team it is meant to help has failed, whatever the data says.

---

**A national historic environment record**

A long-term digital transformation for a public sector body, migrating all current and historic heritage data into a customised Arches HER instance. More than 200,000 records, moved without data loss. Site visits, workshops and detailed data modelling on the human side; Kubernetes, Python in the cloud and Ray data workflows on the technical side.

**Tomorrow's Cities**

Arches deployed and hosted as the data repository for a large cross-disciplinary research programme on urban policy and disaster risk, spanning institutions in Scotland, Nepal, Kenya and Turkey, with UKRI-compliant data management. This meant interviewing social scientists and geologists alike to arrive at one shared set of requirements.

**London Science Museum**

We extended Arches' Bulk Data Manager so that users could edit, flag and bulk-upload data themselves, and are contributing that work back into the wider Arches codebase.

**Maritime Mile**

Cultural heritage and immersive visualisation work on Belfast's waterfront.

**A state government heritage register**

A public-facing heritage register delivered for a state government department, now live and in daily public use.

We also host the international Arches developer meetup, and have run community events combining Arches with virtual reality to push what the platform can do.

It is a fair question, and one we would rather answer than dance around.

The software is free. What is not free is moving twenty years of records into it without losing anything. Nor is the data model that has to reflect how your organisation actually works, the integrations, the hosting, or someone who answers in year three when the person who set it up has moved on.

That is what you are buying, along with the fact that we have done it before at both ends of the scale.

* **Registered Arches providers**, working directly with the Arches core team. We ran the workshops that built a roadmap for integrating Arches with modern DevOps practice, and we were the leading team doing so.
* **We host the international Arches developer meetup.** If you want to judge how seriously we take this platform, that is a reasonable place to look.
* **We contribute back.** Improvements we build for one organisation go upstream wherever they can, which is also why the platform you are adopting keeps getting better.
* **We have worked at national scale and at small-team scale**, and we do not confuse the two. What a 200,000-record national register needs is not what a small record office needs.
